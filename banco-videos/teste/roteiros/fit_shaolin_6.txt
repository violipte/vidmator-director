Deep Squat

Many people walk through life disconnected from their foundation, losing the natural ability to sit deeply, move freely, and maintain balance. This deep squat is not just an exercise; it is a rediscovery of your inherent power. To truly fix yourself, you must first find your center by touching the earth. Your foundation dictates your strength in all things, physical and spiritual. This movement will improve your lower body strength, restore vital hip mobility, and sharpen your balance for every daily movement, from picking something up to climbing stairs.

Stand tall, feet shoulder-width apart, toes pointing slightly outward. Begin by rooting your feet firmly into the earth. Feel that unwavering connection. Your spine is long, your chest open, ready. Inhale deeply through your nose as you initiate the movement, sending your hips back and down as if you are lowering yourself into the deepest chair you can find. Keep your heels glued to the ground, your knees tracking in line with your toes. Descend with control, aiming to bring your hips below your knees, maintaining a straight, proud spine. Do not rush. This is not about speed, but about mastery.

As you exhale, press powerfully through your rooted feet, rising back to your starting position. Each repetition is an act of discipline, a moment of profound presence. Your body is a training ground for your mind. Feel the tension, embrace the effort, and remember that strength is built from the ground up, inside and out. Master this fundamental movement, and you build an unshakeable base for all challenges. Complete 15 controlled repetitions.

Push-Up

Many people lack the fundamental strength to truly support their own body, to push away obstacles with power and stability. Your upper body and core often remain untrained, leaving you vulnerable, unable to fully harness your natural strength. The Push-Up fixes this. Meet resistance head-on, for true power emerges from steadfast effort. This exercise builds the foundational power that allows you to push through challenges, both physical and mental.

Begin in a high plank position. Your hands are directly under your shoulders, fingers spread wide, gripping the earth. Your body forms a single, straight line from the crown of your head to your heels. Feet are rooted, active. Take a deep breath in through your nose as you slowly lower your chest towards the ground, keeping your elbows tucked close to your body. Control this descent; do not collapse. Feel the stretch in your chest, the tension building. Your core is a solid stone, preventing any sag in your hips or arch in your lower back. This is not just a movement; it is a meditation. You are fully present in every inch of the lowering.

Now, exhale powerfully through your mouth as you press the earth away from you, pushing yourself back to the starting position. Extend fully, but do not lock your elbows. This concentrated effort forges discipline. Each repetition is a testament to your resolve, a training ground not just for your muscles, but for your mind. You are teaching your body to support itself, to move with purpose and controlled power. Embrace the burn. Embrace the challenge. This is how strength is built, rep by dedicated rep.

You will perform 12 repetitions.

Plank

Many people struggle with a weakened core, leading to poor posture and often, nagging lower back pain. You spend hours sitting, hunched, allowing your body's central pillar of strength to diminish. This lack of stability leaves you vulnerable to injury and limits your power. The Plank is your forge, strengthening your core, improving your posture, and creating a shield against back pain.

To perform it, begin on your forearms and knees. Extend your legs straight back, one at a time, until your entire body forms a single, unyielding line from the crown of your head to your heels. Your elbows are directly beneath your shoulders, forearms parallel or hands clasped. Actively press the floor away with your forearms. Engage your core deeply; imagine drawing your navel powerfully towards your spine. Your gaze remains down, neck neutral, avoiding any strain. Crucially, do not allow your hips to sag towards the floor, nor to pike up towards the ceiling. Maintain that unwavering straight line. Your feet are rooted, pressing through the heels. Throughout this hold, breathe deeply and steadily through your nose. Do not hold your breath; let it flow, calming your resolve.

Stillness, you see, holds immense power. This exercise is not about movement, but about cultivating an unyielding core, a foundation of strength that can withstand all storms, both physical and the challenges of life. Your core is your center, your anchor. By training this physical center, you train your mental fortitude. Every tremble, every burning sensation, is not a sign of weakness, but an opportunity to deepen your resolve, to witness your mind's desire to give up and choose to persist. Your body becomes a profound training ground for your will. Strengthen your center, strengthen your spirit.

Hold for 45 seconds.

Leg Raise

Many lack true control over their core and the powerful muscles that initiate movement from the hips. This leads to instability, inefficient energy, and limits your dynamic potential. The Leg Raise is not merely an exercise; it is a profound lesson in rooting your power. You will develop an iron lower abdomen and precise hip flexor control, vital for every jump, kick, and agile transition. Learn to lift from your depths; controlled movement begins with a powerful, stable root, extending from your center.

To begin, lie flat on your back, pressing your lower spine firmly into the ground. Your hands can rest by your sides, palms down, or gently support your head. Engage your abdominal muscles immediately, creating a stable, unmoving anchor. Keeping your legs straight and together, slowly lift them towards the ceiling. The movement originates not from straining your neck or back, but from the deep strength within your lower core. Lift only as high as you can maintain perfect form, ensuring your lower back never arches away from the floor. Breathe deeply through your nose; exhale as your legs rise, inhale as you slowly, with absolute control, lower them back down. Do not let your feet crash; resist gravity every inch of the descent.

This is a test of inner discipline as much as physical might. Each leg raise is an act of focused intention, a refusal to yield to gravity or mental distraction. Feel the burning in your core; this is the forge where true strength is hammered. Your body is a temple, and this exercise offers a profound demonstration of presence and control. Master this movement, and you master a deeper part of yourself, understanding that true power emerges from a calm, centered will. Do not rush. Be present in every millisecond of ascent and descent. This is your training ground, forging both body and mind. Perform fifteen controlled repetitions.

Forward Lunge

Many people move through life with uneven strength, one leg compensating for the other. This creates imbalances, making you less stable, less powerful. The Forward Lunge is your remedy. It enhances leg strength, refines your balance, and builds unilateral lower body power, ensuring each leg carries its own might with confidence.

Step with purpose, each stride a commitment to progress and balanced mastery. Begin standing tall, feet rooted beneath your hips. Now, take a generous step forward with one leg. As you plant your foot, actively lower your hips until both knees are bent at roughly a 90-degree angle. Your front knee should be aligned directly over your ankle, never extending past your toes, while your back knee hovers just an inch or two above the ground. Maintain a long, upright spine; keep your shoulders relaxed, your gaze steady. Breathe deeply through your nose as you descend, feeling the stretch and engagement. Then, drive powerfully through the heel of your front foot, exhaling as you return with control to your starting position. This is not a race; it is a moving meditation.

Each lunge is a lesson in presence, a test of your inner equilibrium. You are not merely moving muscles; you are sculpting discipline. Feel the subtle shifts, the effort required to stabilize. This physical practice cultivates mental focus. Your body becomes a training ground for the mind, teaching it to find balance even when challenged, to commit fully to each moment. Embrace the challenge, knowing each deliberate movement builds not only physical power but also unwavering mental fortitude.

Perform 10 repetitions per leg.

EXERCISE 6 of 6: Horse Stance (Mǎbù) (60s hold)

Most people crumble when the legs burn, the knees cave, and the mind begs to quit. Weak inner thighs, poor hip stability, and a scattered mind make the body wobble. Horse Stance fixes this. It forges unshakable legs and a calm center. You become hard to move, inside and out.

Take a wide stance, feet past shoulder width. Toes slightly in, heels heavy. Screw your feet into the floor as if twisting the ground apart. Sink your hips until your thighs aim toward parallel. Knees track over toes and press gently outward. Tailbone drops, pelvis neutral, lower belly lightly braced. Spine long, crown lifted, chin tucked. Shoulders soft. Hands make fists at the hips or palms together at the chest. Gaze level. Breathe through your nose. Inhale low into the ribs and belly. Long, steady exhale. Keep your weight centered over midfoot. If you start to pitch forward, root the heels. If your knees drift in, push the floor apart and feel the inner thighs catch. Micro-adjust, never collapse. Stay tall. Still, but alive.

Stand firm as a mountain. Unmoving yet adaptable. The shake will come. Welcome it. Do not fight the fire; shape it with breath. Each exhale is a hammer that forges resolve. Discipline is not loud here. It is quiet, deliberate presence. You learn to hold pressure without panic. This is Shaolin: the body as a training ground for the mind. When your legs want out, choose one more breath. Then another. The ground holds you. You hold yourself.

60s hold.

Make these six your daily ritual. Five steady breaths in the Deep Squat, heels heavy, hips open, spine long. Eight to twelve Push-Ups, hands under shoulders, ribs down, neck neutral. A 30 to 60 second Plank, forearms pressing the floor, glutes tight, breath through the nose. Ten slow Leg Raises, low back kissing the ground, toes long, exhale to lower. Six Forward Lunges each side, knee tracking toes, back heel tall, pelvis square. Then Horse Stance, Mǎbù, 60 to 120 seconds, feet rooted, knees wide, tailbone heavy, crown lifted.

One to three rounds. Most days. Not maxed out, just present. Consistency over intensity. You don’t chase fatigue. You cultivate precision. You finish with more breath than you started.

You don’t overcome chaos. You become the still point inside it.

Start today. One round. Five minutes. Bare feet on the floor. Nose breathing. Eyes soft. Do it again tomorrow. And the day after. Let the reps become a promise you keep. Simple. Quiet. Relentless. In time, the problems get small. You get steady. You become the monk of your own temple.
