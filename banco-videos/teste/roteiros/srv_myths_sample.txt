Listen up, rookie. Lost in the wild? "Stay put!" they yell. Sounds smart, right? Wrong. Sometimes, staying put just means you're waiting for hypothermia, starvation, or a bear to find you first. Blind adherence to that myth? That's how you die. I lost this eye learning that lesson. We'll ditch the bad advice and dig into the real science of when to move, and when to truly stay put. Your life depends on knowing the difference.

Alright recruits, listen up. Myth one: you're parched, surrounded by snow. Hollywood tells you, just scoop it up, right? Wrong. That's a fast track to trouble, rookie.

Your body's a furnace. When you shovel frozen water in, it kicks into overdrive trying to melt and warm it. That takes energy, burns precious calories, and guess what else? It uses your body's water reserves for that metabolic effort. You're trying to hydrate, but you're actually dehydrating yourself faster. Plus, you're chilling your core. Hypothermia, remember? I almost lost this eye to bad advice once, trying to drink frozen water. Never again.

So what do you do? Simple. Melt it. Use a pot, a bottle near a fire, even your armpit in a pinch. Warm it up. Drink liquid water. Not ice. Got it?

Alright, listen up, rookies. Myth number two: a swig of booze'll warm you right up in a cold snap. Hollywood loves this one. Guy's freezing, takes a slug of whiskey, suddenly he's got fire in his belly. Pure garbage, and it'll get you killed.

Here's the science. Alcohol makes your blood vessels expand, especially those close to your skin. That's called vasodilation. Feels nice and toasty, right? Blood rushes to the surface, makes you feel warm. But that's exactly the problem. You're dumping heat out into the environment, fast. Your core temperature, the one that keeps your organs running, drops like a stone. Had a guy once, thought he was clever, trying to "stay warm" on a patrol. Next morning, he was colder than a penguin's lunch. I lost this eye learning that the hard way. Not that exact way, but you get my drift.

The brain gets fuzzy too. Your judgment goes out the window right when you need it most. You strip off layers because you feel warm. That's a death sentence.

So, listen close. If you're cold, you need to insulate, shelter, and refuel with actual food. Alcohol is a liability, not an asset. Understood? Good.

Alright, rookie, Myth Number Three. And this one's a real brain-buster for you Hollywood types. It’s the idea that you should ration your water down to a few sips a day if supplies are low. Tough it out, right? Be a hero, clench your jaw, ignore that dry throat.

Listen up. That’s how you die. Fast. Your brain, your muscles, your blood - they need water, constantly. Every cell. You start getting thirsty, your body is already screaming. Deny it, and your judgment goes first. You can't think. Can't move. You become useless. You won't be finding more water, you'll be stumbling around like a drunken pigeon, dehydrating quicker than if you just drank what you had.

I lost this eye learning that lesson, trying to 'conserve' the last drop of coffee. Don't ask. The point is, your body is the resource. Keep it functioning. Drink when you're thirsty if you have the supply. Your priority isn't saving water, it’s finding more. Rationing water to the point of severe thirst just cripples your ability to do that. Understand? Don't be a dummy. Keep the fluid in the tank.
