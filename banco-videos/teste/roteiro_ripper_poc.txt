In the autumn of 1888, terror gripped the streets of Whitechapel, London. A killer the world would come to call Jack the Ripper murdered at least five women, and was never caught. Detective Inspector Frederick Abberline led the largest manhunt the city had ever seen. Among dozens of suspects was a young barrister named Montague Druitt, whose body was pulled from the river Thames just weeks after the final murder. More than one hundred and thirty years later, the case remains unsolved, one of the greatest mysteries in criminal history.
