Number one. Eliminate the noise before it enters.

Most people surrender the best hour of their day before they have even decided to be awake. The phone is already in your hand. Someone else's emergency is already reshaping your nervous system. Someone else's opinion is already occupying real estate in your mind that belongs to you. You do not notice this because it has always been this way, because the habit is older than your awareness of it, because the discomfort of stillness feels worse in the moment than the slow, invisible damage of constant input. You have been trained to believe that a quiet mind is a wasted mind. It is one of the most expensive lies modern life sells.

Marcus Aurelius, governing an empire that stretched from Scotland to Syria, still found a way to begin in silence. Not because he had time. Here is how you apply it. Before your feet touch the floor tomorrow morning, do not reach for the phone. Place it across the room tonight so that reaching it requires standing up, which requires a choice, which is exactly the point. Sit on the edge of your bed or move to a chair. Set a timer for five minutes. Do nothing. Do not meditate perfectly. Do not visualize. Do not plan. What you will discover after several days of this practice is not dramatic. It is quiet. A small but unmistakable sense that you arrived at your own life before anyone else got there first. Number two. Define precisely what you want.

Vagueness is comfortable because it is safe. If you never name the exact thing, you can never be exactly wrong about it. You can want more money without specifying an amount. Number three. Surrender the outcome without abandoning the effort.

Number four. Use voluntary discomfort to prove your own power.

Number five. Guard the images you allow in your mind.
