If you own one of these five motorcycles, do yourself a favor, and never sell it. These aren't the flashiest bikes on the road, and they'll never win a bar fight over horsepower. What they do is rarer. They start every single morning, they cost almost nothing to run, and they'll still be worth real money a decade from now. These are the five best motorcycles you can actually live with, day in and day out, here in the United States.

Number five, the Honda Rebel 500. On paper it's a small cruiser, but that misses the point. The Rebel makes riding feel easy. A low twenty-seven-inch seat lets almost anyone flat-foot it at a stoplight. The 471cc twin is smooth, forgiving, and returns close to seventy miles per gallon. It's light enough to flick through downtown traffic and stable enough for the freeway home. Honda reliability means your maintenance bill is basically oil and tires. It won't scare a new rider, and it won't bore an old one.

Number four, the Kawasaki Ninja 400. Don't let the sportbike looks fool you. This is one of the most usable machines ever built. At around 366 pounds, it's feather-light, so parking, filtering, and low-speed turns stop being stressful. The 399cc twin makes just enough power to be genuinely fun without ever getting you in trouble, and it barely drinks fuel. Insurance is cheap, parts are everywhere, and the engine is famously bulletproof. New riders learn on it and never outgrow it. It's the definition of a keeper.

Number three, the Suzuki SV650. This one is a legend for a reason. That 645cc V-twin has been refined for over twenty years, and it makes the kind of low-end torque that makes real-world riding effortless. No need to scream the engine just to get moving. It's comfortable, upright, and endlessly forgiving. Mechanics love it because almost nothing breaks. And here's the kicker. Suzuki holds its value like few bikes on Earth. Riders put fifty thousand miles on them and sell them for nearly what they paid. If you find a clean one, you don't sell it, you pass it down.

Number two, the Honda CB500X. If you could own only one motorcycle for the rest of your life, this might be it. It's an adventure-style bike, with a tall, comfortable riding position, a bit of wind protection, and suspension that soaks up America's cratered roads. That same 471cc twin returns seventy-plus miles per gallon and will run past a hundred thousand miles on basic care. Commute on Monday, strap on the luggage and disappear for the weekend on Friday. Comfortable, efficient, and nearly indestructible.

And number one, the Yamaha MT zero seven. Ask a hundred experienced riders for the best all-around motorcycle, and this is the name that comes up again and again. The heart of it is the 689cc twin, with a torque curve so wide and so friendly it flatters every rider who touches it. It's light, cheap to run, mechanically stone-reliable, and fast enough to keep any rider grinning for years. It commutes, it carves canyons, it tours, all of it well. There's a reason these barely depreciate. The people who own them refuse to let them go. If one ends up in your garage, keep it.

So there it is. Five motorcycles that don't just survive daily life, they make it better.
